# DeepSeek-V4: the indexer projections must be wide, not packed (2026-09-12)

## The defect: a policy whose meaning depended on the file it met

`Ds4DenseQuant` states, per call site, what may happen to a dense weight. It had two values, and one of them did
not say what its call sites meant.

`kKeepWide` means "never requantise; take the packed-or-fp16 policy unchanged". Three call sites used it for the
indexer projections (`indexer_proj`, `indexer_compressor_kv`, `indexer_compressor_gate`) and the code states the
reason at length: their output feeds `ds4_indexer_score` -> `ds4_indexer_topk`, a **discrete** top-k over
compressed entries, where a perturbed score changes *which* entries are attended, not merely by how much. The
comment calls keeping them wide "a mitigation ... the one place where the mitigation is free."

That is what `kKeepWide` delivers on an **F16- or F32-dense GGUF**, where `ds4_dense_keeps_packed` is false and the
flag falls through to the fp16 expansion. It is not what it delivers on a **Q8_0-dense GGUF**. There
`keeps_packed(kQ8_0, K)` is true, so the same flag resolves to "keep the file's packed Q8_0 blocks":

1. They land on `dense_packed<kQ8_0>`, the per-element decode kernel.
2. The site's stated *reason* no longer describes what it gets. (It does not follow that the weights are too
   narrow — see "What this change is, and is not" below. The precision argument is what motivated the
   investigation; it is not what justifies the change.)

ggml-org's `DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731` MXFP4 GGUF — the non-abliterated model this engine is now tuned on — is
Q8_0-dense. Measured cost, prefill profile of one 2048-token chunk, two cards (scratchpad `ds4_pp_kprof.out`):

| kernel | ms (two-card) | share of GPU | calls | ms/call |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `ds4_dense_packed@attn` | 935.7 | 21.5% | 126 | **7.43** |
| `ds4_dense_f16@attn` | 160.8 | 3.7% | 550 | **0.29** |

126 calls is exactly 21 indexer layers x 3 tensors x 2 cards. A 25x per-call gap, for a precision nobody asked for.
Neither a throughput number nor a perplexity gate reports this: both files load, both answer correctly, one is
silently far slower at the wrong precision.

## The change

`Ds4DenseQuant::kWideF16` — "never requantised **and** never packed: materialise fp16" — at the three indexer
sites. `kKeepWide` keeps its meaning and its one correct user, `token_embd`, whose packed residency is 1.06 GB of
deliberate VRAM saving and whose gather reads one 8 KB row per token, so fp16 there buys nothing.

The placement rule is now a pure function, `ds4_dense_route(dt, K, Ks, quant) -> {kPacked, kRequantised, kFp16}`,
which `upload_dense` calls and `deepseek4_dense_route_test` pins without a GPU, a queue or a model. The `K`/`Ks`
asymmetry is preserved: whether a dtype *has* a packed form is a property of the whole row, whether *this card's*
column slice is a whole number of blocks is a property of the slice.

No kernel changed. `dequant_rows` already fed the fp16 expansion from Q8_0, and `dense_w` already dispatched
`d.p` -> `d.q8` -> `d.w`.

### What this change is, and is not

It is a **performance** change that costs a little precision. The phase was written believing the opposite, and
the gate corrected it; the record should carry the correction rather than the original reasoning.

`dense_packed` decodes Q8_0 as `d(fp16) * qs(int8)` accumulated in fp32 — at most 19 significant bits into 24 — so
the packed route was **bit-exact to the file's own values**. The fp16 image rounds them to 11 bits, about 2^-11
relative, against the file's own ~2^-8 quantisation error: roughly an eighth more of an error already present.
Widening cannot recover precision the file never stored, so on a Q8_0-dense GGUF this does not deliver "the
precision the call site was avoiding" — that precision *is* the file's.

Taken purely on precision, a Q8_0 source is better served by `kAuto`: yesterday's full-file scan showed the
requantiser recovers `d` and `qs` bit-for-bit on these exact tensors (172,032 + 688,128 + 688,128 blocks, zero
mismatches), the decode GEMV keeps activations and accumulator in fp32, and it would **save** ~46 MB instead of
spending ~50, at the cost of one conversion pass per prefill chunk. `kWideF16` is chosen here for the prefill
kernel it buys. It remains the precision-correct choice for F16/BF16 sources, where `kAuto` adds real loss.

## Measured (two cards, DS4_TP_GPUS=0,1; scratchpad ds4_idxwide.log, ds4_idx_kprof.out)

**Throughput**, like-for-like pairings only:

| | before | after | |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| pp.2048, profiled | 579.6 | 675.6 | **+16.6%** |
| pp.2048, unprofiled (8-length stress) | 585.7 | 686.3 | **+17.2%** |
| serve prefill, 14,612-token prompt | 463 | 518 | +11.9% |
| tg.128 / tg.4096 | 32.67 / 26.05 | 32.91 / 26.90 | +0.7% / +3.3% |
| two-card prefill GPU | 4360.747 ms | 3468.907 ms | **-891.84 ms (-20.45%)** |

`ds4_dense_packed` appears in **0 rows** of the new kernel table. `dense_f16@attn` absorbed the 126 calls while
its per-call time *fell*, 0.2924 -> 0.2413 ms; those two rows net -933.4 ms, and about +41.6 ms reappeared in
`attention_xmx`, `indexer_score` and `cast_f32_f16`, which is why -933 became -892. Decode also gains because the
same 126 calls ran there (1.607 ms of a 40.089 ms two-card budget), damped by decode being expert-DMA-bound.

**Quality — and the reason the strongest-looking evidence is worth the least.**

Every likelihood dump came back byte-identical to the pre-change reference: batch (PPL 3.9476, avg NLL 1.373110),
stream (3.9514 / 1.374068), each reproduced twice, all on data rows. The criteria had predicted a small move and
allowed +-0.5%.

**That result was guaranteed, not observed, and the code said so at the lines this change edits.** Both quality
runs used `--max-tokens 512`. The model's `indexer.top_k` is 512, and a ratio-4 CSA layer emits about `ctx/4`
compressed entries, so at 512 tokens there are ~128 candidates against a top-k of 512: `ds4_indexer_topk` admits
every one of them and the discrete stage never selects. No perturbation of the scores could have changed the
output. The comment immediately above two of the changed call sites states this and ends: *"Do not read the
identical result as evidence that requantising these is safe; read it as evidence the experiment was blind. The
4096-token pair is the one that would settle it."*

Worse than missing it: the phase's own criteria predicted the dumps *would* differ. An exactly-zero delta against
a predicted-nonzero one is a falsified premise, and it should have prompted the question instead of ticking the
box.

**The real long-context evidence is the serve run, which the phase produced and did not claim.** Its prompt is
14,612 tokens, roughly 3,650 compressed entries against a top-k of 512, so the indexer is genuinely selecting
there. The 64 greedy tokens came back md5 `688f407b7c` — unchanged from the pre-change route, and identical across
both requests. That is real evidence at a length where the discrete path is live. It is coarser than a likelihood
dump: it shows only that the argmax never flipped, on one prompt, over 64 tokens. It lowers the residual risk
substantially without closing it.

**Settling run (09:26-09:35): the control is exact, and selections flip only once the selector selects.**
Stream PPL over 4096 tokens, pre-change binary (worktree build of 09e24bd, `ie-ds4-ppl` md5 `0aaa499aed6f`) against
this one. The two runs scored the same tokens row for row (target ids compared). Per-token deltas are split at the
crossover where the discrete stage starts choosing: `top_k` is 512 and a ratio-4 layer holds ~`ctx/4` compressed
entries, so it admits everything below ~2048 tokens.

**Percentages below are perplexity-relative**, matching criterion 6's band; mean NLL is given alongside because the
dumps are in nats. (The same data quoted NLL-relative reads -0.50% live and -0.18% whole-run — a different
convention, not a different result.) "Moved" counts an *exact* inequality; where a 1e-9 tolerance changes the
count, both are shown.

| regime | tokens | moved (exact / >1e-9) | worse / better | > 1 nat | mean abs delta | median | mean NLL | PPL |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| **blind** (< 2048, control) | 2048 | **0 / 0** | 0 / 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1.047798 -> 1.047798 | 2.8514 -> 2.8514 (**+0.0000%**) |
| **live** (>= 2048, the result) | 2047 | 2001 / 1977 | 1042 / 959 | 21 | 0.06707 | 0.00046 | 0.598108 -> 0.595097 | 1.8187 -> 1.8132 (**-0.30%**) |
| all | 4095 | 2001 / 1977 | 1042 / 959 | 21 | 0.03353 | 0 | 0.823008 -> 0.821503 | 2.2773 -> 2.2739 (-0.15%) |

**The control is the strongest fact here and it is exact.** Not "zero within a tolerance" — zero moved tokens at
exact inequality across all 2048 tokens below the crossover, with the first nonzero delta anywhere appearing at
position **2094**, just past it. That forecloses the alternative reading: nothing in this change perturbs anything
except through the indexer's selection, and it does so only once the selection is real.

The live half is the result, and it says plainly that **this change is not output-preserving at long context**:
about 97% of tokens move once the top-k is genuinely choosing. The movement is small and two-sided — 1042 worse
against 959 better, median 0.00046 nats, 21 tokens past 1 nat. That is selection churn, not degradation.

The prior long-context measurement at deepseek4.cpp:440-455 is the yardstick: 3071 tokens under a **full Q8
requantisation**, a perturbation roughly 8x larger than this one's ~2^-11, reporting 35 tokens past 1 nat, 1704
worse / 1367 better, mean abs delta 0.077, and net **-0.25% PPL**. Its statistics were aggregated over all 3071
tokens while only ~1023 sat in the live regime, so its live-regime figures are about 3x quoted: mean abs delta
~0.23 and ~3.4% past 1 nat, against this change's 0.067 and 1.03%. An 8x smaller perturbation producing ~3.4x less
churn needs no explanation beyond the fp16 rounding. On the directly comparable number this change is slightly
larger than the yardstick, -0.30% against -0.25% PPL, at a fraction of its per-token dispersion.

The net direction on this text is mildly favourable, and no weight should be put on that sign: one corpus, and a
symmetric perturbation lands where it lands. The defensible claim is **magnitude and symmetry**, not improvement.

**Cost and safety:** resident weights 5.38 -> 5.43 GB per card (+~50 MB — the three roles are 49,545,216 elements,
not the ~24.8 M the criteria estimated, so x 0.9375 B/element = 46.4 MB, which the measurement reflects); expert cache unchanged at 82 slots/layer
and 23.570 GB. 8-length stress at ctx 4096 (129/512/1023/2048/3000/512/64/4000) clean, faster at every length, no
DEVICE_LOST/OOM/nan. The abliterated F16/F32-dense model is a genuine control — its indexer tensors are **F32**, so
its route was and remains `kFp16` — and its dump stayed byte-identical.

## Two builder errors caught during the phase, recorded

1. The validation script omitted `IE_QUEUE_PROFILING=1`, so `--kprofile-prefill` emitted no kernel table and the
   check "`ds4_dense_packed@attn` ABSENT" was a false positive of the grep. Re-run with profiling on; only that
   re-run is evidence for the 0-call claim.
2. The first speedup figure reported (+19.4%) compared a new **unprofiled** run against a **profiled** baseline.
   The like-for-like figures are +16.6% (profiled) and +17.2% (unprofiled).
3. The quality criterion was written at a context length where its own subject could not be exercised, and the
   result was read as confirmation rather than as the falsified prediction it was. The gate caught it; see the
   quality section. The rationale for the change was also stated backwards and is corrected above.

Criterion 7 as written ("decode within 2%") is exceeded on the upside at tg.4096 (+3.3%) with the mechanism named
above; it was not re-specified after the data.

## What this suggests to look for elsewhere

The bug class is a per-call-site policy that resolves through a *file* property. Any other flag of that shape
deserves the same question: does it still mean what its call sites meant when the file changes? `kKeepWide`'s one
remaining user was checked and is correct.
